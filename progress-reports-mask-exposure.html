Progress reports that mask exposure

A progress percentage can look healthy while cost and time risk build up underneath. Verified progress, based on accepted work, tells the real story.

3 MIN READ

Many monthly reports show steady progress against plan. Then, late in the project, a large overrun appears almost overnight. In most cases the warning signs were there all along. The report simply measured the wrong thing.

Installed is not accepted

Installed, inspected and accepted quantities are three different numbers. Work can be installed but fail inspection. It can pass inspection but still wait for formal acceptance. Approval of a supplier or a sample does not accept the installed work.

When a report counts installed work as progress, it can overstate completion and hide the rework still to come.

Site activity becomes credible progress

We treat progress as a four-step sequence:

  1. Ready: access, information, supply and resources are in place.
  2. Execute: sequence, workfronts and contractor coordination.
  3. Inspect: inspection and test plans, traceability and hold points.
  4. Accept: authorized disposition and closeout evidence.

Only work that has completed the cycle counts as verified progress.

Reconcile progress with cost and time

Verified progress is then reconciled with the remaining cost, delivery constraints and the completion forecast. This shows the true exposure early, while there is still time to act.

Clients receive a readiness register, a look-ahead, verified progress, NCR trends and a handover register. Together they replace optimism with evidence.

Report what has been accepted, not what has been attempted.
