Receipt, review, approval and authorization are different events

Many site errors start in the document register, not on site. Controlled information means everyone knows which version is current and what it may be used for.

3 MIN READ

A drawing arrives from the designer. It is logged, circulated and filed. Weeks later, the contractor builds from it, only to find it was never approved for construction. Or a newer revision was issued, but the old copy stayed on the workfront.

Four steps, four different statuses

  1. Identify: number, package, discipline and revision are assigned on receipt.
  2. Review: a responsible reviewer is named, with a due date and recorded comments.
  3. Authorize: the document is given a status and a permitted use, such as information, tender or construction.
  4. Distribute: recipients, transmittals and revision history are tracked.

Receiving a document is not reviewing it. Reviewing is not approving. Approving is not authorizing it for construction. Each is a separate event with its own record.

Withdraw what is superseded

When a new revision is issued, superseded copies are withdrawn from the workfront and the audit trail is kept. This simple discipline prevents some of the most expensive rework on any project.

One reference across the project

Through the project management information system (PMIS), common package references connect RFIs, submittals, NCRs, changes and risks to the programme, the cost forecast and the decision register. Permissions, backups and export rules are agreed at the start, so the information can be handed over intact.

The same discipline builds the handover archive progressively: as-builts, tests, operating manuals, training records and warranties, ready when the owner needs them.

If you cannot tell which drawing is current, you cannot control what gets built.
