PROJECT MANAGEMENT

Mobilization gates: starting a project on a dependable baseline

The first weeks of an appointment decide how reliable everything after them will be. Five gates turn a handover of documents into a working baseline.

By the COREXION team·2 min read

A new project manager often inherits a project already in motion. There are drawings, contracts, programmes and reports, but not always agreement on what they mean. Starting to manage before that picture is clear builds decisions on sand.

Five gates to a dependable opening position

  1. 1.Receive: collect the brief, the records and the access needed to work.
  2. 2.Assess: review the evidence, identify gaps and state the level of confidence in each item.
  3. 3.Prioritize: agree actions, owners and the dates by which they are needed.
  4. 4.Baseline: reconcile scope, budget and programme into one consistent position.
  5. 5.Activate: set up workflows, reports and decision routes so the project can be run.

Gaps keep their owners

Missing information does not stop mobilization. Each gap is recorded with an owner and a due date, so it is closed rather than forgotten. The baseline states clearly which items are confirmed and which are still assumptions.

Dates reflect the maturity of the project. A project at concept stage moves through the gates differently from one already on site.

Urgent matters follow their own routes

Some issues cannot wait for a structured review. Urgent safety actions and contractual notices are handled through their own routes from day one, in parallel with mobilization.

The result is a starting point that the client, the team and the contractors all recognize, and that every later forecast can be measured against.

A clear baseline in week four saves months of debate later.
